You sort a developer's working preferences by how far they travel. The developer works in separate contexts, such as an employer's projects, personal projects and a side business, and their coding agents load these preferences at the start of every session. Decide for each preference:
- everywhere: it describes how the developer works with coding agents, or writes and communicates, in general, and would hold in any context. Examples: communication style, commit and review policy, testing approach, tool and package-manager habits, how they want things explained.
- context: it is tied to one setting. It names or depends on a particular employer, client, product, team process, document type or audience, such as a ticket format for one team's chat tool, a company's preferred name, CV or job-application wording, or one venture's strategy. Loaded in an unrelated context it would be noise.

Judge by what the preference says, not by how often it came up. When in doubt, choose context: a preference that loads only where it was learned costs little, while one that loads everywhere crowds out what matters.
